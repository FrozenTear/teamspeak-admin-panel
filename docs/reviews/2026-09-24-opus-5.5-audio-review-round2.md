## TL;DR

1. **Verdict on "wake latency, not contention": right about where, not
   quite right about how.** I agree it is host-side and that contention
   on cores 0-1 is not the cause. Fencing 0-1 is withdrawn. But the
   dominant mechanism in the data is not a steady 2 to 4 ms cost per
   wake. It is the VM losing large, uneven chunks of wall time on every
   vCPU. `/proc/stat` accounts only 74% (Angerfist), 60% (Bubble Pop)
   and 55% (Rick) of wall time on each vCPU during the plays, and 82 to
   86% while idle. Per 6-second census interval, the share of time lost
   this way predicts the share of time in 100 ms+ send gaps with
   Pearson r = 0.92. The fit is gap share ≈ 0.003 + 0.86 × lost share,
   so with no lost time there are essentially no gaps. This is what an
   oversold shared-core KVM host with no steal clock looks like.
2. **The 2 to 4 ms per wake does not compound into 100 ms gaps.** A
   frame costs about 3 to 4 serial wakes. At 2 to 4 ms each that is 8 to
   16 ms, inside the 20 ms budget, and the 32-frame channel absorbs
   jitter. The per-slice average is the mean of mostly short waits plus
   a few hundred very long ones. voice-rt's total wait (164 / 84 /
   126 s) is the same order as the time spent in 100 ms+ gaps
   (140 / 80 / 88 s). The tail causes the gaps, and the average is a
   symptom of the tail. The 32-frame catch-up bursts come after a gap.
   They make what listeners hear worse but do not create the gap.
3. **Pop tracks are not worse because they are pop.** They make no
   more wakes (voice-rt 131 to 138 wake-ups/s against 160), but each
   wake waited 1.8 to 2.6x longer (2.9 to 4.2 ms against 1.6 ms),
   and the VM lost 33 to 38% of its time against 23% for Angerfist. The
   host got busier from 09:59 to 10:06, and the pop tracks played then.
4. **Ranked fixes:** (1) a dedicated-vCPU host, (2) Floki only if it
   passes the same measurement and the music API gets auth, (3) Voice's
   inline voice send plus a stale-frame policy, (4) the split wire task
   as the new default, (5) `cpuidle_haltpoll.force=1` as a cheap
   diagnostic with a small gain, (6) spin-before-frame last. Only (1)
   and a verified (2) attack the cause. Items (3) and (4) help on any
   host but will not make Contabo clean.
5. **Steps 2 to 5 should sit on the step 1 line.** If the host reading
   is right, every step keeps gap share within about ±0.03 of
   0.86 × (lost share measured in the same window), whatever the code
   change. A step that drops clearly below the line is a real fix
   effect. The census must run during every step. Otherwise host drift
   (which moved lost share from 0.23 to 0.38 within 20 minutes on step
   1) will look like fix effects.
6. **The cheapest decisive extras:** a 60-second hidden-steal probe (a
   pinned spin loop that records clock jumps its own thread never
   switched out for), per-thread worst-case wait from
   `/proc/<pid>/task/<tid>/sched` or a small bpftrace script, the
   kernel's accounting config, and a `tcpdump` wire trace so step 4
   keeps a per-frame trace after the WARN is gone.

### 1.3 The census, re-derived, with two new findings

Voice's `sch.py` reduction (window-edge deltas) reproduces exactly: voice-rt run / run_delay
130.6 / 169.7 s (Angerfist), 45.2 / 83.7 s (Bubble Pop), 74.3 / 134.0 s
(Rick). Wait per slice is 1.65 / 3.82 / 4.26 ms, and cores 0-1 are 86
to 90% idle. The Bubble Pop figures are corrected for the sampler bug
in 1.2; before the fix they read 61.0 / 127.5 s and 4.04 ms. The idle baseline before play is 1.56 ms wait per slice
for voice-rt, against 0.18 ms for the unpinned `tokio-rt-worker` threads.

**New finding A: the guest does not account for a quarter to half of
wall time, on every vCPU.** Summing all `/proc/stat` fields per CPU
across each window and comparing with wall time:

| Window | Accounted share, cpu0 / cpu1 / all six | Busy share, all six |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idle, 09:37 to 09:49 | 0.82 (all) | 0.07 |
| Angerfist | 0.74 / 0.74 / 0.74 | 0.08 |
| Between tracks, 10:00:06 to 10:00:22 (not playing) | 0.41 (all) | 0.10 |
| Bubble Pop | 0.60 / 0.60 / 0.61 | 0.08 |
| Rick | 0.55 / 0.55 / 0.55 | 0.08 |
| Idle after, 10:08 to 10:10 | 0.86 (all) | 0.07 |

For the same windows, the run time `schedstat` charges to threads pinned
to 0-1 (voice-rt plus ack-sender) is 1.8x, 1.9x and 2.7x the total busy
time `/proc/stat` reports for cores 0-1. Both cannot describe the same
real CPU time. Tasks are being charged run time for periods the kernel's
tick accounting never saw. That is what happens when the host
deschedules a vCPU that is running a guest task and the guest has no
steal clock (the census shows steal 0 and kvm-clock).

Caveat: this reading assumes the guest kernel accounts idle time
precisely (`CONFIG_NO_HZ_IDLE`, the distro default). On a tickful-idle
kernel, a halted vCPU the host does not reschedule could also lose
ticks. Section 6 lists the one-line check.

**New finding B: lost time predicts the gaps, interval by interval.**
For the 142 census intervals (about 6 s each) with playback:

| Relation | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Pearson r, gap share vs lost share (cpu0, cpu1, or all CPUs) | 0.92 |
| Least squares | gap share = 0.003 + 0.858 × lost share, residual sd 0.073 |
| Pearson r, loop-stall seconds per second vs lost share | 0.91 |
| Lowest quartile of intervals | lost 0.07, gap 0.07 |
| Highest quartile | lost 0.56, gap 0.49 |
| Per-track means (lost / gap) | 0.23 / 0.20, 0.33 / 0.31, 0.38 / 0.34 |

The bot's own activity is constant during play (50 frames/s, about 150
voice-rt slices/s). The lost share still swings from 0.07 to 0.56, and
it is 0.06 to 0.32 per minute while idle and 0.59 between tracks. So
the lost time is external and the gaps follow it, not the reverse.

Other census facts worth keeping:

- 22% (Angerfist, Bubble Pop) and 36% (Rick) of voice-rt slices end
  in an involuntary switch. With the CPUs 90% idle, the likely
  preempters are the pod's own threads on 0-1: the ack-sender, pool
  threads from `block_in_place` handoffs, and sibling/loop wakes. This
  self-preemption is real but cheap unless the vCPU is then
  descheduled.
- voice-rt grew to 43 to 51 live tids per window (127 thread starts in
  the 19-minute log). That matches round 1: `block_in_place`
  blocking-pool churn with a 10 s keep-alive.

## 2. Verdict on the wake-latency reading, and the 2 to 4 ms vs 100 ms gap

**Where I agree with Voice.**

- It is host-side. Cores 0-1 are about 90% idle, the other-task census
  is light, and softirq is under 1.3%. Round 1's H1 split between
  "contention on 0-1" and "hidden steal". The contention half is
  refuted, and the fencing proposal in round 1 is withdrawn.
- There is a real per-wake floor. Idle voice-rt waits 1.56 ms per
  slice, against 0.18 ms for unpinned threads that usually land on
  busier (so non-halted) vCPUs. `cpuidle` driver `none` means every
  idle vCPU halts, and waking it needs the host to reschedule it.
  haltpoll addresses exactly this part.

**Where I differ.** The per-wake floor cannot produce 100 ms+ gaps on
its own, and the data point at a different, larger mechanism:

1. **No compounding path from the averages.** Per frame, the wake chain
   is timer to sibling, sibling to loop (mpsc wake of a parked worker),
   `block_in_place` core handoff, and `poll_send_acks` to ack-sender.
   voice-rt runs about 150 to 163 slices/s and the ack-sender about 45
   to 49/s, so roughly 3 plus 1 per frame. At 2 to 4 ms each that is 8
   to 16 ms, under 20 ms. The loop is also decoupled from the pacer by
   the 32-frame channel, so a slow chain would show as steady queue
   growth, then channel-full underruns. Drains take about 1 ms for 20
   frames (round 1, section 1.2), so service time inside a burst is
   tiny. A gap needs one long wait, not many short ones.
2. **The tail is large enough to be the gaps.** Total voice-rt wait is
   164 / 84 / 126 s, and total time in 100 ms+ gaps is 140 / 80 / 88 s.
   The wait total is an upper bound on the tail, since waits overlap
   across 45 threads, but the orders match. A mean of 1.65 ms over
   102,552 slices is what you get from about 100,000 sub-ms waits plus
   a few hundred waits of 100 to 700 ms. The poll-leg freezes and pacer
   oversleeps (up to 718 ms) are those long waits seen from inside.
3. **Halt-wake latency should not create lost accounting time.** A
   halted vCPU is idle in guest accounting, so waiting for the host to
   wake it shows up as idle, not as missing time. Missing time comes
   from vCPUs descheduled while running or mid-transition, which is
   hidden steal. Finding B ties the gaps to missing time, not to idle
   time.

**Net reading:** the gaps are episodes where the host takes the vCPUs
away for tens to hundreds of ms. The per-wake floor exists but is a
second-order cost. Section 5 gives the test that separates the two: if
haltpoll or split wire lower the wait per slice but gaps stay on the
0.86 × lost line, descheduling dominates.

**What the 32-frame burst adds.** `send_audio` only queues
(`vendor/tsclientlib/tsproto/src/connection.rs:462-470`). After any
pause, three catch-up mechanisms stack:

- The sibling paces against an absolute schedule
  (`crates/voice/src/audio.rs:1000-1043`), so every frame whose slot
  passed during the pause is released at once. That is the 30 to 51%
  already-late share.
- The sibling-to-loop channel releases its backlog of up to 32 frames
  (`audio.rs:50`).
- The loop pushes them all into `acks_to_send`, which is flushed as one
  burst at the next events poll (`connection.rs:615-637`).

Together that gives 29 / 43 / 47% back-to-back sends. The TS client
jitter buffer holds 20 to 60 ms (`docs/voice/0001-latency-budget.md:34`),
so a 640 ms burst is either dropped or played late, and the listener
hears the gap plus a skip or a latency jump. The burst also asks for CPU
right when the VM comes back. It does not create gaps, but a
stale-frame policy (section 4, fix 3) would turn "gap plus smeared
burst" into a clean gap and keep listener buffers stable.

**Pop tracks under the per-wake theory (seat item 5).** If per-track
content mattered, the pop tracks would need more wakes per second. They
have fewer:

| | Angerfist | Bubble Pop | Rick |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| voice-rt wake-ups/s (first-to-last sighting, 1.2) | 160 | 131 | 138 |
| voice-rt wait per wake | 1.63 ms | 2.93 ms | 4.19 ms |
| decode-rt wait per slice | 1.29 ms | 2.46 ms | 3.04 ms |
| tokio-rt-worker wait per slice | 4.2 ms | 9.8 ms | 7.7 ms |
| Lost wall-time share | 0.23 | 0.33 | 0.38 |
| Gap share | 0.22 | 0.37 | 0.41 |

Every thread class waited about 1.8 to 2.6x longer during the pop tracks,
including decode-rt on 2-5. The lower full-buffer share for the pop
tracks (87 and 81%) fits: the producer was starved too. The shorter
tracks were not the cause. They simply played when the host was busier
(per-minute lost share peaked at 0.46 to 0.52 between 09:59 and 10:06,
against 0.18 to 0.32 during most of Angerfist). Cheap
confirmation: replay Rick at a quiet time, or run the three tracks in
reverse order with the census on. Rick should then land on the same
line with Angerfist-like numbers.

**Talk power (seat item 1) remains unknown.** A logical fork for
Voice's packet check: tsclientlib's `intern_can_send_audio`
(`vendor/tsclientlib/tsclientlib/src/lib.rs:1042-1066`) only runs the
talk-power test if the bot's book has the channel with
`needed_talk_power` set. If TS6 never sends `channel_needed_talk_power`
on the voice protocol, that branch is skipped and returns true, so the
WARN would have to come from a flag. But ServerQuery `clientlist` shows
all five flags clean. Either the voice protocol tells the bot something
different from what `clientlist` shows, or the talk-power branch is
live. Either way the answer is in the bot's own `notifycliententerview`
and `channellist` lines. That needs a one-off debug dump of the
own-client and own-channel book fields, or `log_commands(true)` for one
reconnect. Both are small code or config changes, so they need a
HOLD-MERGE PR and the owner's go, not a read-only check.

## 6. Cheap measurements that would settle it faster

All are read-only in the sense of changing no configuration. Two add a
small, short-lived load; that is noted.

1. **Kernel accounting config (zero cost).**
   `grep -E 'CONFIG_(NO_HZ_IDLE|NO_HZ_FULL|PARAVIRT_TIME_ACCOUNTING|IRQ_TIME_ACCOUNTING|VIRT_CPU_ACCOUNTING_GEN)=' /boot/config-$(uname -r)`,
   `dmesg | grep -iE 'kvm-stealtime|steal|clocksource'`, and
   `cat /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpuidle/current_driver`. With
   `NO_HZ_IDLE=y` and no steal-time line, lost accounting time means
   descheduled-while-running (section 1.3 caveat).
2. **Keep computing lost share (zero cost).** The census already reads
   `/proc/stat`. Per interval, lost share =
   `1 - sum(fields 1..8 delta) / (100 × seconds × ncpu)`. Also read the
   per-CPU lines in `/proc/schedstat` (field 7 is the run time of all
   tasks on that CPU). Where that exceeds `/proc/stat` busy on the same
   CPU, tasks were charged run time the tick never saw.
3. **Hidden-steal spin probe (one vCPU for 60 s).** Pin a tight loop to
   one core that reads `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` and records every jump over
   1 ms together with its own `nivcsw` from `/proc/thread-self/status`.
   A jump with no involuntary switch is time the vCPU itself did not
   run. Run once idle on core 5 and once during a play on core 5, not
   on 0-1, to avoid perturbing the bot. `jitterdebugger` or
   `cyclictest -m -a 5 -t1 -i 1000 -D 60 -h 1000 -q` from rt-tests
   gives the same with a histogram. Accept a host at max under 2 ms.
4. **Worst-case wait per voice thread (seat item 8).** Two options:
   - If `cat /proc/sys/kernel/sched_schedstats` is already 1:
     `grep -E 'wait_max|wait_count|sum_exec_runtime' /proc/<pid>/task/*/sched`
     gives each thread's worst runqueue wait since start, with no
     overhead.
   - Otherwise, a bpftrace script with small overhead:

```
tracepoint:sched:sched_wakeup, tracepoint:sched:sched_wakeup_new
/ args->comm == "voice-rt" || args->comm == "tsproto-ack-sen" /
{ @q[args->pid] = nsecs; }

tracepoint:sched:sched_switch
{
  if (args->prev_state == 0 &&
      (args->prev_comm == "voice-rt" || args->prev_comm == "tsproto-ack-sen")) {
    @q[args->prev_pid] = nsecs;      // preempted while runnable
  }
  $t = @q[args->next_pid];
  if ($t) {
    $d = (nsecs - $t) / 1000;         // microseconds
    @hist_us[args->next_comm] = hist($d);
    @max_us[args->next_comm] = max($d);
    if ($d > 50000) { printf("%llu %d %s wait_ms=%d\n", nsecs, args->next_pid, args->next_comm, $d / 1000); }
    delete(@q[args->next_pid]);
  }
}
```

   Join the printed long waits (monotonic ns) with the send-gap trace.
   If most 100 ms+ gaps start with one long wait, the tail reading is
   confirmed. Guest timestamps include host descheduling, so combine
   with probe 3 to split guest-side from host-side waits. Similar
   output: BCC `runqslower -p <pid> 50000`, or
   `perf sched record -a -- sleep 60` and then
   `perf sched latency --sort max`.
5. **A wire trace that survives step 4.**
   `tcpdump -i any -n -tt -q 'udp and dst port 9987 and greater 80'`
   on the Contabo host for the play window. Voice packets are the
   larger ones at 50/s. It records the real wire time (after the
   `acks_to_send` flush), shows the catch-up bursts on the wire, and
   gives step 4 a gap trace with no WARN.
6. **Order control for the pop-track question.** Replay the three
   tracks in reverse order in one of the later steps, with the census
   on.

### 7.5 Is the growing voice-rt thread count a factor?

Mostly a symptom, and a useful gauge; it is not a cause.

- On the single loop, every frame calls `block_in_place`
  (`bot.rs:688-689`, `audio.rs:1391-1392`). That hands the worker core
  to a blocking-pool thread, so the worker role rotates across pool
  threads.
- tokio only spawns a new pool thread when no idle one is available. A
  finished handoff thread becomes idle only once it gets CPU to park.
  So the pool grows when handoff threads are not being scheduled.
- Idle threads exit after 10 s. So the live count (43 to 51 per step 1
  window, about 62 at the end of step 2) tracks recent scheduling
  delay. It fits step 2 being a worse host period from the start.
- As a cost: each spawn is a clone, a `sched_setaffinity`, a
  `setpriority` and two log lines (tens of µs), and idle threads use no
  CPU. More distinct threads rotating the worker role does worsen wake
  locality, but that is second order.
- It is not a leak. Please say whether "62 tids" was live threads at
  one moment or cumulative starts; only the live count carries the
  gauge meaning.
- Step 3 removes `block_in_place`, so the count should stay near 4. If
  gaps stay on the line with 4 threads, thread growth was not a factor.

## 9. Review of PR #81 (Voice's inline flush and catch-up cap)

This reviews the real diff on `cursor/voice-inline-flush-0b43` (commits
`832d4e7` and `a2fc43b`, +916 −73 across `crates/voice/src/audio.rs`,
`crates/voice/src/bot.rs`, and the two vendored files) against fix 3 in
section 4. Nothing was pushed to #81 or commented there. Summary:
correct and safe with the flag off. Two real issues with the flag on: a
cap of 0 silences the bot, and the cap measures the wrong age, so it
will miss the bursts that matter most on this host. The flush part
removes no wake-ups.

### 9.1 The vendored hook: correct, no races

- `tsproto::Connection::poll_flush_outgoing` (`connection.rs`, +23) is
  a public wrapper around the existing `poll_send_acks`, and
  `tsclientlib::Connection::poll_flush_outgoing` (`lib.rs`, +22)
  forwards to it when connected. Additions only; existing code is
  unchanged.
- **No race with the poll leg.** Both paths need `&mut Connection`. The
  single loop and the wire task each own the connection and call the
  flush and `events()` from the same task, so they cannot interleave
  mid-drain. Both pop the same FIFO, so nothing is double-sent or
  skipped.
- **Packet-id ordering is unchanged.** Voice packet ids are assigned at
  encode time in `send_packet`, in enqueue order. The flush drains
  FIFO, so wire order is unchanged.
- **Acks and resends are unchanged.** Commands and inits never enter
  `acks_to_send`; they go through `Resender::send_packet`
  (`connection.rs:462-470`), and resend and ping stay on `poll_next`.
  The flush does send queued acks earlier, which is harmless or
  slightly helpful. The one pre-existing hazard is kept, not made
  worse: on a full ack-sender channel (256), `try_send` silently drops
  the packet while counting it as flushed.
- **Waker handling is right.** `inline_flush` uses
  `std::future::poll_fn` with the calling task's waker. On the fallback
  socket path, a `Pending` send registers the task that also polls the
  connection, so no wake-up is lost.

### 9.2 The inline flush does not remove a wake-up in steady state

The PR's premise is that `send_audio` "only enqueues" and that the send
waits for a later wake-up. In the current loops that is not the case in
steady state:

- After the audio arm body, both loops go back to the `biased`
  `select!` in the same task poll. With no further frame queued, the
  audio arm is `Pending`, so the events arm is polled immediately.
- tsclientlib's `poll_next` calls `con.client.poll_next_unpin`
  (`tsclientlib/src/lib.rs:1188-1189`), and tsproto's `poll_next` runs
  `poll_send_acks` first (`connection.rs:630-637`).
- So the frame reaches the ack-sender within the same wake-up, a few
  µs later. The step 1 logs agree: `voice_poll_leg` lines typically
  show `send_acks_flushed=1`, the one voice frame per poll.

The flush only waits while the loop drains a backlog, because the audio
arm stays ready until the queue is empty. Even then it waits only for
the drain itself, which is about 1 ms for 20 frames (round 1). The
ack-sender still gets one `unpark` per flush, so its per-frame wake stays
too. Step 2 showed that wake is on the send path.

**Prediction:**

- voice-rt wake-ups: unchanged within ±10% against the same-mode
  baseline.
- ack-sender wake-ups: unchanged or slightly up, because a drain now
  flushes per frame instead of once.

The useful mechanical check is that `inline_flush` `flushed_calls`
should be about equal to frames sent, and `send_acks_flushed` in
`voice_poll_leg` lines should fall to about 0 (acks only). That shows
the flush is doing the handoff, while also showing it replaced an
existing handoff rather than a wake-up.

If Voice wants the wake-up saving in fix 3, it needs the other half of
my plan. Send voice inline with a non-blocking `send_to` on a dup'd
socket, falling back to the queue on `WouldBlock`, so the ack-sender is
not woken per frame at all.

### 9.3 The catch-up cap: correct in its own terms, but it measures the wrong age

Correctness:

- `catchup_batch_with` returns the message untouched and does not read
  the queue when disabled (tested). When enabled, it drains contiguous
  frames with `try_recv`, stops at the first non-frame so a
  `PipelineEvent` or `Finished` keeps its order behind the kept frames,
  and drops only the oldest frames down to the newest `N` (tested,
  including a trailing end-of-stream).
- A single late frame is never dropped for `N` of 1 or more, because
  `len.saturating_sub(N)` is 0, so the hot path cannot starve.
- A send error stops a batch, so a trailing `Finished` is not treated
  as a clean end.

**Issue 1: `VOICE_MAX_CATCHUP_FRAMES=0` silences the bot.**
`parse_max_catchup_frames("0")` returns 0 (tested as intended), and
with `N = 0` the hot path drops every frame, including on-time ones
(`frames_behind = 1 > 0`, drop `1 − 0`). Reject 0 (keep the default),
or treat 0 as "cap off". An env typo should not mute the bot.

**Issue 2: age is measured from `enqueued_at`, not from the frame's
schedule.** `enqueued_at` is stamped by the sibling at hand-off
(`audio.rs:1094-1099`), after its pacing sleep. The cap therefore sees
two things only: frames that queued up behind a stalled send loop, and
queue depth.

It does not see the other catch-up, from the pacer itself. After a host
pause the sibling was frozen too. On resume, every frame whose slot has
passed returns from `sleep_until` at once and is encoded and handed off
back to back, each with a fresh `enqueued_at`. That is the 30 to 51%
already-late share. The wire task drains them about as fast as they
arrive, so the queue stays at 1 or 2 frames with ages near 0, and the
cap never fires.

Section 8.5 showed the split already removed most loop-side delay (loop
stalls 35 to 45% below the host line). So on the recommended split path,
the bursts left are mostly pacer catch-up, which this cap cannot see.
Fix: carry the frame's slot (`f.scheduled_at + paused_total`) or its
lateness in `AudioMsg::Frame` and age frames from that, or drop in the
sibling before encode, which also saves the Opus encode for skipped
audio. That is the stale-frame policy in fix 3.

Smaller points:

- `frames_sent += dropped` keeps the progress clock honest, but it can
  jump over a multiple of 50, so one `Progress` tick is skipped. That is
  cosmetic.
- Dropped frames never reach `SendTimingMonitor::observe`, so the
  attribution and `c_loop_deferral` statistics lose exactly the worst
  frames. Do not compare C_loop figures across cap on and off.
- The `inline_flush` counters are process-wide atomics shared by every
  bot. The per-bot figure is `dropped_catchup_frames` on
  `audio_send_summary`.
- The config is read once through `OnceLock` on first use, not strictly
  "at process start". Same effect: it needs a restart to change.

### 9.4 `dropped_catchup_frames` on `audio_send_summary`

Cumulative per play (like `total_frames`, and not reset per window),

**`try_flush_outgoing` (vendored tsproto `connection.rs`, tsclientlib
`lib.rs`): correct.**

- **Queue walk.** It walks `acks_to_send` front to back. On a
  successful `try_send_to` it fires `SendUdpPacket`, runs
  `handle_loss_outgoing`, then pops. That is the same bookkeeping as
  the existing inline fallback, and a popped packet cannot be sent
  again by `poll_send_acks`. `WouldBlock` stops the walk and leaves the
  rest queued. `EINTR` is retried.
- **Resend, acks, packet ids: unaffected.** Init, Command and
  CommandLow never enter `acks_to_send` (they go through
  `Resender::send_packet`), and `handle_loss_outgoing` is statistics
  only. Voice ids are assigned at encode time and not touched. Acks
  owed for inbound commands are still sent inline from the receive
  path (8.6).
- **Waker claim: correct.** tokio's `UdpSocket::try_send_to` goes
  through `try_io` and registers no waker. On `WouldBlock` it clears
  write readiness, and the next `poll_send_to` re-arms it. Doc nit: tokio
  keeps separate reader and writer waker slots, so the default trait
  impl's no-op waker would only have clobbered the writer slot, not the
  recv registration. The override is still the right choice.
- **Ordering.** Within the direct path, order is preserved (it stops at
  the first packet that would block). Across paths it is not strictly
  preserved. After a `WouldBlock`, the events arm hands the rest of the
  queue to the ack-sender thread asynchronously, and the next frame's
  direct send can overtake a packet still sitting in that channel.
  This only happens when the socket send buffer is full, which is rare
  for UDP, and TS receivers order voice by packet id, so it is minor.
  Optional hardening: after a `WouldBlock`, skip direct sends until the
  queue has fully drained through the normal path. Either way, fix the
  "order is preserved" wording.
- **Partial send.** It fires the event, then errors without popping,
  as Voice notes. The packet then goes out whole through the ack-sender
  path, so the event fires twice for one packet. Linux never partially
  sends a UDP datagram, so this is harmless.
- **Errors are non-fatal.** `inline_flush` counts them and warns once,
  and the packet is delivered by the normal path. A persistent error
  (for example a firewall `EPERM`) would therefore degrade silently to
  the old path. `flush_errors` is already in the 1 s `inline_flush`
  line; watch it in the A/B.

corrects my 9.2.** With direct `send_to`, voice packets no longer go to
the ack-sender thread at all. That thread falls to acks that hit

### 10.4 How S4 gaps will be judged once lost share arrives

ack-sender wake latency (2 to 4 ms per wake here), and a pause that
hits only the ack-sender makes a wire gap with no log gap. For 100 ms
gaps the difference should be small but positive. Back-to-back shares
also differ, since one ack-sender drain can emit several queued frames
together.

## Appendix: reproduction notes

- Recount: per track, the window is bounded by the first muted WARN
  after `first_frame_on_wire` and the next `AudioCommand::Play`. Gaps
  come from consecutive muted-WARN timestamps. Already-late share sums
  `already_late_frames / window_frames` over `pacer_wakeup` lines.
- Lost share: census `cpu*` lines, deltas between the samples nearest
  each window edge (as `sch.py` does). Accounted = sum of the first
  eight fields / 100 (USER_HZ). Busy = user + nice + system + irq +
  softirq.
- Interval regression: consecutive census samples with at least 100
  frames between them, 142 intervals.
- Healthcheck enrichment: `an.py` with gaps limited to under 5 s.
